# Showroom update — 10 September 2026

Seven things were added in this release. The database migration only adds
tables and columns; nothing existing is dropped, renamed or truncated, so your
cars, accounts and history come through untouched.

## What is new

**Photos on every car.** Open a car and add photos from your phone, marked as
"when bought", "on arrival", "repair" or "for selling". The arrival ones are
the ones that matter: weeks later, when a supplier or a shipper says the car
left him in perfect condition, they are your only answer — so an arrival photo
on a car that has been sold cannot be deleted. The first photo shows beside the
car in the showroom list. Files are checked by their content, not their name,
so nothing but a real JPG, PNG or WEBP is ever stored.

**Deposits: holding a car for a buyer.** He pays something now and comes back
with the rest. The money goes into the account you choose immediately — it is
real cash in your hands — but it is not profit, because the car is not sold.
While the deposit stands, the system will not let that car be sold to anybody
else. When he comes back, the deposit counts towards the price and is *not*
collected twice. If he walks away, you either give it back (a line takes it out
of the account) or keep it, and keeping it is reported as income rather than
being left unexplained in the cash box. This is Rule 11 in `docs/money-rules.md`.

**Printed paper.** Every sale has a receipt to hand the buyer: what he bought,
the agreed price, every payment received, and what is still owed, with space
for both signatures. Nothing about your cost or profit appears on it. Every
account statement prints too — it puts your business name and the balance at
the top and drops the buttons.

**Profit by supplier.** One row per supplier: cars sold, what they sold for,
what they really cost, how much of that was repairs, the profit and the margin,
how often his cars arrive damaged, and how long they take to sell. A supplier
whose cars arrive needing work is not cheap, however low his price, and this is
the page that shows it.

**Cars that are losing money.** A car whose cost has climbed past its asking
price is flagged while it is still in the garage or the showroom — when you can
still do something about it — instead of on the day you sell it. The dashboard
warns as soon as one appears.

**Expected freight against the invoice.** When you ship a car you can record
what the shipper quoted. When his invoice arrives, the difference is kept, per
shipment and per company, so a shipper who quotes low and bills high stops
being invisible.

**Every rate you have actually paid.** Not a published rate — the ones your own
shipments locked and your own wires used, on one line over time, with the best,
the worst and the average you have paid on transfers.

**More figures on the dashboard.** Profit month by month for the last year
(months that lost money are red), where every unsold car is standing and how
much money is tied up at each stage, what you owe in dollars and in francs on
separate scales, deposits held, and what customers still owe you. Every chart
has its numbers underneath for when you need the exact figure.

## Updating an existing installation

1. Back up your database and your existing application folder.
2. Stop the application. Replace the application files, keeping your own
   `server/.env` **and your `server/uploads` folder** if you already have
   photos. Do not copy a demo database over your data.
3. From the project folder:

```bash
npm ci
npm run db:migrate
npm run generate --workspace=server
npm run build
npm start
```

The migration adds the photo and deposit tables and one nullable column for the
quoted freight. Run it before starting the new application.

For a new installation, follow START-HERE.txt / `npm run setup`.

## Where the photos live

Photos are written to `server/uploads/cars` on disk, not into the database — a
database full of photos becomes slow to back up, and a backup you avoid running
is not a backup. Include that folder in whatever you copy off the machine.
